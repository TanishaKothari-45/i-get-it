## 1. The job

When I catch myself saving yet another "I should learn this" while three others sit untouched, I want to put tonight's 20 minutes into one of them and have something to show for it, so I can stop collecting interests and start finishing one.

Other moments it happens:
- The tutorial ends and nothing in my week has changed. Work version: the competitor tracking is still done by hand (heard from one Orbitshift PM, 3 Oct).
- A 60-second clip hooks me and I want more than the clip: WWII, western philosophy, a film (Prateek, 4 Oct).
- A new video or release lands and my existing plan feels stale. Strongest in AI, where it happens weekly (Prateek, 4 Oct).

Who, by situation (not age, city or title): someone who wants to do a lot and spreads thin. Has saved dozens of "learn this" posts and follows creators who teach, hoping the content resurfaces; it drowns in the feed. Has started at least one of these and stopped. Would say "I keep meaning to learn X" without being asked. Work version of the same person: on a team where colleagues have started shipping agents, with a weekly chore an agent could take over.

Today they hire: the saved folder and watch-later (most common, zero follow-through). Following the creator and hoping. A course bought on sale, abandoned at lecture 4. ChatGPT when stuck, re-pasting context every time. A friend who did it, one message then nothing. Most often: nothing, and the next scroll.

More than one person in the product: two people share the same moment and differ at the end.
- The Orbitshift PM: tries the thing on their own chore, so the chore stops being done by hand. **Built for this weekend.**
- Prateek and the GrowthX coursemates: try the thing on their own agents or dive into a topic, so they have an earned POV on the hype, not a shared reel. Second ring.

What needs doing: turn tonight's 20 minutes into one real step on one of the three things, with something to show at the end.
How they want to feel: moving, not collecting. Progress they can point to. No guilt about the folder.
How they want to look to others: the one who has actually done it, not the one who shares reels about it. An earned POV on the hype.

The bench: not B2B this sprint. The person with the interest is the person who signs in.
The one we serve first: a PM on the Orbitshift Product Ops team with a weekly manual chore an agent could take over. Five committed, nine in reach. Coursemates next.

Checked 4 Oct against the three tests: product out of the sentence, still makes sense (no app, path or tutor in it); a moment I could watch (the save button, three half-started things behind it); one "so I can". Weak spot, on record: the last clause is Prateek's wording of "spreads themselves thin", not yet a coursemate's words. Fix: say the sentence to two coursemates this week and write down what they say back.

Shape decided 4 Oct (evening): the product is a generated handbook for the thing they typed, modelled on the GrowthX Build Sprint handbook itself. Outcome first ("by day 7 you'll be able to X"), 7 chapters of about 20 minutes, each one teaches one thing and checks it inside the app. Building or practising in the real world is offered on each chapter, never required, never checked. Our strength is generating the pedagogy, not policing the doing. Parked for later, named so they aren't lost: regenerate the plan with comments (progress kept), change level or language mid-path, a tutor chat scoped to the mission, a second and third topic with a forfeit to add a fourth, Job B (a 2 to 3 hour taste of a topic).

## 2. The switch

What they'd fire: saving it for later. Doing nothing now, while the save feels like progress. The handbook's "including nothing" case, and the most common one.

The forces that matter (two of four; pull is weak on its own and habit is the thing being fired):

Push, outside them: "I started noticing people using it in multiple ways." Everyone around them is already into the thing; the gap shows. Heard twice: Prateek on Jev (4 Oct), and the Orbitshift PM on colleagues starting to ship agents (3 Oct).

Anxiety, the risk of starting: "There was a pile of stuff I'd accumulated and this felt like starting in the middle and glossing over the rest. It felt like skipping levels, doing something without a broader plan." (Prateek, 4 Oct.) The pile doesn't just sit there; it makes any start feel illegitimate.

Pull, inside them (kept, but it isn't what moves them on its own): be the one who has actually got it, with an earned POV. From section 1.

Habit (named so we build against it, not for it): save → feel like progress → next scroll. Free, instant, in hand.

What the product does about each:
- Push -> chapter 1 on their topic, passed, inside five minutes of typing it. Not a plan about it, a first thing learned and checked tonight.
- Anxiety -> a plan before the first lesson: "By day 7 you'll be able to X. Seven chapters. Tonight is chapter 1." If they say they know some of it: "Start at chapter 3; here's 1 and 2 in a line each." Starting is no longer skipping levels, because the levels are on the screen.
- Habit -> ask for less than a save buys. A save costs nothing and gets nothing. One line and five minutes gets a plan and a first chapter passed. Why they pay that at this moment and not before: the push. The night they notice others using it is the night the effort is affordable, and the plan turns the pile into an order instead of a weight. (Reasoning, not yet heard from a user.)

The one worry onboarding must remove: "If I start here I'm skipping levels; I don't have a plan." So the first screen shows the plan, then chapter 1, in that order.

## 3. The core flow

Today (what they do now, before the product; from Prateek's Jev story, 4 Oct, to be corrected from one Orbitshift PM's last attempt on Monday):
1. 11pm, phone in hand, scrolling. A clip on something new catches them.
2. They save it. Maybe follow the creator, hoping it resurfaces.
3. They scroll on. The save felt like progress. (The habit.)
4. Days later they notice people using it in multiple ways. Now they want in. (The push.)
5. They open YouTube and watch a tutorial, 20 to 40 minutes.
6. The tutorial ends. They think about going further. The pile looms: "starting here is skipping levels, I don't have a plan." (The anxiety.)
7. They decide to do it properly first: search for a roadmap or a course. Save that too.
8. Not done. Weeks later the thing is mainstream, and they still only have the clip.

With my product (night 1, the first session):
1. 11pm, phone in hand, scrolling. A clip catches them. Thumb over the save button. (The flow starts here, before the product.)
2. They open the link instead and type the thing in one line. "Jev's agent thing." "Swimming." "WWII." A URL is accepted and its title becomes the topic; nothing is parsed or promised. One tap for level: new to this, or know some. (Language: one field in the same call, default English.)
3. It shows the handbook for it: "By day 7 you'll be able to [outcome]." Seven chapters, one line each, chapter 1 marked tonight. Days 14 and 28 named as the horizon in one line each, not generated. The one picture for the whole topic (the analogy the week hangs on) is on this screen.
4. Chapter 1 opens. Short cards, in the handbook's order: the one picture, teach one thing, a worked example, a common mistake. Reading, not watching: no video is the lesson. Exercises are interleaved, not stacked at the end: the first one comes by about card 3.
5. The exercises, inside the app, checked on the spot: guess before the reveal, apply it to a small scenario, recall. Two or three per chapter. Feedback names what they confused with what, never "incorrect". A miss gives one re-teach card and another go; the rung moves only on a pass. For doing-topics, one card offers "try it in your own tool tonight" with the smallest version; it's never checked.
6. The rung lights: "Chapter 1 of 7 on [topic]: done. You can now [chapter 1's outcome]." Chapter 2 is named once, as the open loop.
7. Job done: a plan they didn't have an hour ago, with one rung on it, tonight.

Things it takes to get the job done today: 8, and the job never gets done · With my product: 5 (open, type, read the plan, do chapter 1, see the rung).

What must not happen:
Step 2: a messy or vague line must not bounce them to a form. One clarifying question at most, then a plan.
Step 3: the plan must not be plausible-sounding nonsense for a topic the AI knows thinly. Always seven chapters; when the topic is too wide or too thin for seven honest ones, it narrows the topic with the one clarifying question, it never shortens the plan or invents facts. (Resolves the "7 or fewer" contradiction from the first review.)
Step 4: a chapter must not be a reading list or "watch this". It teaches one thing in under ten minutes of reading, in the handbook's shape.
Step 5: an exercise must not test anything the chapter didn't teach. A miss must not block, must not say wrong, and must not move the rung.
Step 5: closing the tab mid-chapter must not lose the place. Reopening lands on the same card.
Step 6: the night must not vanish. The plan and the rung survive a refresh, a closed tab and a reopen on the same phone.

Other flows (stop when the job is covered):
- Night 2 onwards: open, two or three recall exercises from earlier chapters, then chapter N. Same shape minus the plan reveal.
- Regenerate with comments (nice-to-have): "more on X, less on Y, go deeper here." Done chapters are kept; the rest regenerates.
- Tutor chat scoped to the mission (nice-to-have): the Shaktimaan role, reads the plan and the rungs.

## 4. Onboarding

First value (the moment it first does the job for them): the first exercise of chapter 1 passed, on their topic, about five minutes after typing it. That's the first observable proof that they've started, and the product checked it. The plan before it is reassurance, not proof; chapter 1 done, about 20 minutes in, is the night's "something to show".
The smallest commitment we ask for: one line typed and one tap (level). No email, no account, no install, no name.
The worry it removes (from section 2): "If I start here I'm skipping levels; I don't have a plan."

From opening the link to the first value:
1. Opens the link on their phone at 11pm, from a colleague's message or the launch post. removes: "another app to install?" It's a web page.
2. Sees one line and one box: what this is, and "What do you keep meaning to learn?" removes: "what do I do here?"
3. Types one line, taps a level, taps go. removes: "this will want my email first."
4. Sees the plan: "By day 7 you'll be able to X", seven chapters, tonight marked. Reassurance. removes: "I'm skipping levels, I don't have a plan."
5. Reads chapter 1's first cards, on their topic. removes: "it'll be generic."
6. Passes the first exercise. The first value, about minute five. removes: "I'll just be reading again."
Then the rest of chapter 1, the rung, chapter 2 named.

Login: not before the rung lights. After chapter 1 is done: "Keep this plan? Sign in, and pick when tomorrow's 20 minutes happen." Because the first night has to work with nothing given, and because the question is easy to say yes to once they've seen it work.
What this means for the build: the anonymous night is tied to the device (a token on the phone, a row in Convex) so "reopen lands on the same card" holds without an account. Sign-in attaches that row to the account; nothing is re-asked.
What we don't ask on day one: name, why they want it, how much time they have, notification permission, a tour, other topics.
What we ask later, and when: "when tomorrow?" right after sign-in. Comments on the plan when they tap "change this plan" (nice-to-have). A second topic when they type one.

## 5. v1

Does (the must-haves: one person finishes night 1, and comes back for night 2; "the night plus coming back", decided 4 Oct):
- Type one line (or a URL; title becomes the topic), tap a level, and get a 7-chapter plan with "by day 7 you'll be able to X", days 14 and 28 named as the horizon, chapter 1 marked tonight.
- Chapter 1 generated for that topic in the handbook's shape: the one picture, teach, worked example, common mistake, with two or three exercises interleaved and checked in the app.
- Feedback that names the confusion; one re-teach card on a miss; the rung moves only on a pass.
- "Chapter 1 of 7 on [topic]: done. You can now [outcome]." Chapter 2 named once.
- The night survives a refresh, a closed tab and a reopen on the same phone, with no account.
- Sign in after chapter 1 (Convex Auth). The night attaches to the account. Pick when tomorrow's 20 minutes happen; stored and shown back ("see you at 9pm"). No message is sent in v1.
- Night 2 onwards: two or three recall exercises from earlier chapters, then the next chapter, same shape. Chapters generate one at a time, the night before or on open.
- After sign-in, the same plan and rungs on any device.
- Works on a phone, logged out until the rung, on someone else's laptop.

Doesn't (not this sprint: parked, not forgotten):
- Share-to-app from Instagram or YouTube. Reading a reel or a video.
- Generating days 8 to 28. The horizon is named, not built.
- A reminder at the chosen time. Needs an email or push provider, which is a new service: ask Prateek before adding one.
- Checking whether they tried it in their own tool. Offered on doing-topics, never gated, never graded.
- Streaks, badges, percentiles, share cards, payments, anything social.

Nice to have (only after the must-haves work, in this order):
1. Regenerate the plan with comments. Done chapters are kept; the rest regenerates.
2. Change level or language mid-path, same mechanism.
3. The tutor chat, scoped to the mission, casual. Reads the plan and the rungs as its memory.
4. A second topic, which brings the three-slot rule and the forfeit with it.
5. "Don't remember" on a recall exercise, counted as a miss, never a block.

How I'll know it worked (what they do again, not what they say): a person who finished night 1 opens it again and finishes chapter 2 within 48 hours, with no reminder sent. Target for the first five: 3 of 5 finish chapter 1 in under 25 minutes without Prateek speaking; 2 of those 3 come back for chapter 2.

## 6. The riskiest guess

If this is false, the product is pointless: given the handbook's chapter shape, the AI can produce, for whatever topic a stranger types, a 7-chapter plan with an honest day-7 outcome and a chapter 1 that reads like teaching written for that topic, with exercises that are answerable from the chapter and checkable by the app without a human. If what comes back is a listicle with a quiz, the plan answers the skipping-levels worry with a lie, and the first night is a reading session they've had before.

Thirty-minute check, no code (run it while you build), and what happened:
- Decided before running. We believe: a chat model given the chapter template does this for most topics. To check: paste the template and 10 one-line topics into a plain chat window, at the "new to this" level: swimming, WWII, western philosophy, Jev's agent thing, an agent that tracks my competitors, read a balance sheet, and four in coursemates' own words. Measure, per topic: (a) the day-7 outcome is specific and honest (not "understand the basics"); (b) seven distinct chapters, no invented facts on a spot-check of two claims; (c) for three of the ten (swimming, agents, WWII), chapter 1 has at least two exercises answerable from the chapter and gradable by a rule or a one-idea rubric; (d) a person reading it can tell it was written for that topic, not pasted from a template. We're right if 8 of 10 pass (a), (b) and (d), and all three pass (c).
- Then two timed people: chapter 1 text on a phone, no help. Pass if both finish inside 20 minutes, pass the exercises unaided, and can say in one sentence what they can now do. Fail if either needs me, can't say it, or says "I've read this before". Failures and what they change get written here.
- Same 10 topics into ChatGPT study mode, scored against the same four criteria. Added 4 Oct on Shaktimaan's condition. If it ties, generation isn't the edge; the plan, the recall and coming back on night 2 are, and "finishes chapter 2 within 48 hours" becomes the real riskiest guess.

## 7. Milestones (each starts "I can", demoable in ten seconds, riskiest first)

1. I can type a topic on the live link and read a generated chapter 1 with its first exercise checked on the spot. (The smallest credible task and its usable result, first.)
2. I can see the 7-chapter plan with "by day 7 you'll be able to X" and chapter 1 marked tonight.
3. I can finish chapter 1: all exercises, a re-teach card on a miss, the rung lit, chapter 2 named.
4. I can close the tab, reopen it on the same phone, and I'm on the same card, no account.
5. I can sign in after the rung, pick when tomorrow is, and my night is attached to me.
6. I can come back, answer recall on chapter 1, and get chapter 2.
7. I can open it on another device after sign-in and land on the same rung.
Last: I can close it, reopen it, and my plan and my rungs are still there.

Acceptance test, separate from the ten-second demos: a stranger, logged out, on their own phone, gets through night 1 without me saying a word.
