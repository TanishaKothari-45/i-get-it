## 2026-10-08

### Decisions

- **D1. Lessons are built from a kit, not a rubric (Prateek).** The plan gives every chapter its blocks (picture, teach, example, mistake, try, move, do it, steps, try it, quiz incl. scenario, watch, one breath) and a proof (set, result, predict, scenario, retell), chosen for this reader and this thing. The five kinds (body, tool, understand, story, decide) are defaults in the prompt, not an enum. Replaced: one chapter shape for every topic (story card, teach, three quizzes). Why: a calisthenics reader said the story time and quizzes were in the way; "a push-up is proved by a push-up". Revisit when a topic needs a block the kit lacks: add a block, not a kind.
- **D2. Body skills pass on a logged set (Prateek).** "Do it" cards log reps, seconds or ticks plus how it felt; the first set passes the chapter. No quizzes. A "try it" page is a bonus, never a wall. Revisit if readers log sets without doing them (a "feel" of easy on every set is the tell).

- **D23. One-sitting handbooks (Prateek, 9 Oct 00:5x, on the live "how to make dal" handbook: "Making dal is barely a 30-minute job. Rules now: (1) a recipe, a single how-to or any one-off task is one chapter, always, every step in it; 2 to 3 chapters only for a recap of a long series or franchise. (2) A one-sitting chapter opens with ingredients or materials (quantities, serves, total time), then numbered steps with timings, the mistakes, a checklist "do it" for mise en place at most; never "move", never a timer, never a logged-set proof; it passes on reaching the end. (3) Existing quick handbooks: exempt from the chapter-1 wall and the daily chapter limits between their own chapters, and every chapter written at plan time, so nothing waits mid-sitting. (4) The live dal handbook is rewritten as one chapter and the library copy replaced, so the next person who types dal never gets the three-chapter one. (5) Screens: no "twenty minutes for chapter 2" reminder, no Tonight/Next tags, a visible "Later" beside every open do-it card. Why one chapter and not "three chapters open the same night": a cook follows one list top to bottom with the pot on the stove; chapter breaks, Done screens and writes in between are interruptions with no teaching value. Why the kit rule: the 8 Oct kit (D1) made body-skill blocks the default for "skill" mode, and a recipe is a skill in the plan's eyes, so push-up-shaped cards landed on lentils. Revisit after 10 one-sitting handbooks are finished: the finish rate against course chapter 1s.
