Totals over 9 chapters: 9 false, 35 misleading (about 1 false and 4 misleading per chapter). Time to chapter 1: 55 to 109 s, typically about 90 s.

Every surviving slip:

- How compound interest works (false): "it would only give you $110 again". Earning a flat 10% on $100 again would bring the balance to $120 at the end of year two, not $110.
- How compound interest works (false): "In year one they're almost identical". In year one simple and compound interest are exactly the same, $50 each, as the same sentence goes on to say.
- How compound interest works (misleading): "Compound interest is a continuous process". This chapter's compounding happens once a year, so calling it continuous gives a beginner the wrong picture.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "your body is either in one mode or the other". The body always burns a mix of sugar and fat, so it is not a clean switch between two modes.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "When sugar is still available, it uses that first.". At rest between meals the body burns a large share of fat even while stored sugar is available, so fat is not simply saved for later.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "During this time, your body isn't burning its own stores". Fat burning drops after a meal but does not stop, so saying stores are not used at all is an overstatement.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "Her liver fills up with glycogen from that meal.". Chicken and vegetables contain little sugar or starch, so this meal adds only a little glycogen to the liver.
- Intermittent fasting (false): "pulls glycogen (stored sugar) from your liver and muscles to keep your blood sugar steady". Muscle glycogen fuels only the muscle itself and cannot be released into the blood, so only the liver's store keeps blood sugar steady.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "After that runs low, your body begins burning fat.". Fat burning rises steadily from the start of the fasted state rather than beginning only once glycogen runs low.
- Intermittent fasting (false): "This stockroom of glycogen lasts roughly 8 to 12 hours". Liver glycogen is only partly used after an overnight fast and usually takes around a day or more to run low.
- Intermittent fasting (false): "After dinner at 7 p.m., it runs low around 3 to 7 a.m.". An ordinary overnight fast uses only part of the liver's glycogen; it does not run low by morning.
- Intermittent fasting (false): "A night's sleep is long enough for the stockroom to run low". An overnight fast does not use up the liver's glycogen; much of it is still there at breakfast.
- Intermittent fasting (misleading): "the stockroom (stored sugar) and then the warehouse (fat)". The body draws on stored sugar and fat at the same time, not one strictly after the other.
- Tenant rights when renting a flat in Bangalore (false): "runs for 12 months or longer has to be registered". Indian law requires registration for leases longer than one year or year-to-year, so a lease of exactly 12 months is not covered by that rule.
- Tenant rights when renting a flat in Bangalore (misleading): "With an 11-month lease, you skip that official record and its extra weight". A properly stamped 11-month agreement is still valid proof in court, so a beginner may wrongly think it gives them weaker protection.
- Tenant rights when renting a flat in Bangalore (misleading): "agreements under 12 months don't have to be registered, but you should still write them down". It mixes up registering an agreement with writing it down, implying the law never requires a written agreement, which is not settled for Karnataka.
- Tenant rights when renting a flat in Bangalore (misleading): "no proof of what you agreed, no way to settle disputes". Spoken agreements still count, and rent receipts, bank transfers and messages are evidence, so disputes can still be settled without a written agreement.
- Tenant rights when renting a flat in Bangalore (misleading): "Without it in writing, you have no proof if something goes wrong". Payment records, receipts and messages can still prove a tenancy, so having no agreement means weaker proof, not none at all.
- Photosynthesis (misleading): "the wood, the leaves, the fruit—comes mostly from the air". Fresh leaves and fruit are mostly water, so only their dry matter comes mostly from the air.
- Photosynthesis (false): "most of the new weight is carbon". Carbon is only about half of a plant's dry weight, and much of a living plant's weight is water, so carbon is not most of the new weight.
- Photosynthesis (misleading): "plants use soil for only a few things". It leaves out water, which plants take from the soil and which makes up a large share of a living plant's weight.
- Photosynthesis (false): "Water provides hydrogen and oxygen for the sugar molecule". The oxygen from split water is released as oxygen gas; the oxygen in sugar comes from carbon dioxide, as card 2 itself says.
- How UPI payments work (misleading): "Your money stays in your bank the whole time.". The ₹100 does leave your bank and ends up at your friend's bank, so 'stays in your bank' contradicts what actually happens.
- How UPI payments work (misleading): "A UPI payment has **four parties**, always in this order.". Apps like Google Pay or PhonePe also work through a partner bank, and NPCI is contacted twice, so 'four parties, always in this order' oversimplifies the path.
- How UPI payments work (misleading): "Meanwhile, NPCI sends a separate message to the receiver's bank". The receiver's bank is told to add the money only after your bank has checked the PIN and taken the money out, not at the same time.
- How UPI payments work (misleading): "if one fails, the money goes back into your account.". Refunds for failed payments are automatic but can take up to a day or more, so beginners may wrongly expect the money back instantly.
- Basics of Carnatic music (misleading): "you don't have one right frequency, you find the spot that sounds clear". A radio station does have one exact right frequency, so the analogy suggests the opposite of how Sa works.
- Basics of Carnatic music (misleading): "To your ear, the melody is identical". A listener clearly hears that one version is higher or lower; the raga is recognizably the same, not identical.
- Basics of Carnatic music (misleading): "It's a small hand pump (or an electronic device now)". The traditional drone is the tanpura, a stringed instrument; the hand-pumped sruti box is only one kind of drone.
- Basics of Carnatic music (misleading): "Each voice chooses its own Sa.". This contradicts card 1: when people sing together they share one Sa, so matching another singer can be right.
- Basics of Carnatic music (misleading): "it doesn't lock everyone to one frequency". When people perform together, the drone does hold everyone to one shared Sa, as card 1 says.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The clergy (the Church), the nobles (landowners with old titles), and the king himself were exempt". Nobles did pay some direct taxes, such as the capitation and vingtièmes, and calling the king a tax-exempt group makes no sense because he was the government collecting the taxes.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The common people—farmers, merchants, craftspeople—who were already paying most of the taxes anyway.". The king's 1787 meeting of notables was called to propose a new land tax on everyone, including the privileged, who refused it.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "Equal taxes sound logical, but in 1788 France didn't work that way yet.". The crown was actually proposing a land tax on all landowners at the time, so marking this option wrong hides what the meeting was for.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The Church and nobles had exemptions by law. The king couldn't just remove them". The crown had already imposed taxes on noble land income, the vingtièmes, so taxing the privileged was not legally impossible, only politically resisted.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "a war that had nothing to do with French survival but everything to do with the king's glory". France joined mainly to weaken its rival Britain and win back power lost in earlier wars, not just for the king's glory.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The court at Versailles was insanely expensive". The court took only about six percent of royal spending, so this suggests a much bigger cause of the debt than it was.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The French crown did neither.". Ministers such as Necker, Calonne and Brienne did try to cut costs and to tax the privileged, but they were blocked.
- The French Revolution (misleading): "The system was designed to transfer money upward to the exempt.". The main taxes went to the crown, not to the exempt groups, and the system grew up over centuries rather than being designed.
- Black holes (misleading): "Throw a ball at 11.2 km per second and it never comes back down.". The chapter never says this ignores air, and a real ball thrown that fast through the atmosphere would be slowed or burned up, so a beginner may think it works literally.
- Black holes (misleading): "to escape Earth's gravity completely and coast toward Mars, it needs 11.2 km per second". Exactly escape speed leaves a craft circling the Sun near Earth's path; reaching Mars needs somewhat more speed than 11.2 km per second.
- Negotiating a salary (misleading): "rent, food, transport, tax, insurance, everything you pay for". Salaries are quoted before tax, but this never says to compare your costs with take-home pay, so a beginner may set a floor that cannot cover their bills.
- Negotiating a salary (misleading): "Her walk-away number is £2,400 a month.". If this is pre-tax salary, Maya's take-home pay would be roughly £1,950, barely covering her £1,800 costs and leaving nothing for her emergency fund; the gross-versus-net difference is never explained.
- Negotiating a salary (misleading): "Then add 20%. That's the number you need to think clearly.". Card 2 says costs plus 20% is only the survival number, not the walk-away number, so this suggests stopping at the bare minimum.
