# I Get It: pitch, pedagogy and one-week scope

Created 2 Oct 2026, engine research added 3 Oct (see docs/adaptive-engine.md, which overrides this file where they differ). Build Sprint, solo. Name: I Get It. Domain: igetit.now (free in DNS as of 3 Oct, not yet bought). Share card line: "I get it now."

## The pitch (one paragraph)

Everyone saves reels and follows creators who teach things, and almost none of it turns into something they can actually do. I Get It is an AI tutor that takes any one skill you want and gets you from zero to your first real result in seven days, twenty minutes a day. It builds the path around the creators you already watch, adapts every day to what you got right, what you skipped and when you actually show up, and turns up on its own with today's lesson instead of waiting to be asked. Mass courses teach everyone the same way. Nobody learns the same way. This learns you.

## What we're betting on

1. The result, not the content. Day 7 ends with a result you can show and the tutor can grade: a thing you built, a talk you gave, a conversation you held, a judgement you made. Shown in the first 30 seconds so you want it. Watching counts for nothing. For art history that's a recorded 3-minute talk on one painting or placing 20 unseen works in their period, not a quiz.
2. The tutor's memory of you is the moat. Skips, misses, time of day, how you answer. ChatGPT doesn't have it and it compounds daily.
3. Proactive beats reactive. The tutor opens the conversation. You never start from a blank box.
4. Creators are the distribution. Their public videos go in the path now, licensed and revenue-shared later.

## First win in five minutes, before sign-up

Time to value is the whole game. The first session has no setup.
- Type the topic. Then one tap: why do you want this? Four options, skippable (defaults to "for the joy of it").
- Within 30 seconds: the 7/14/28 vision written for that motivation, and you're already in the first task.
- The first task is tiny and real. For agents: describe one boring job in a sentence, the tutor turns it into a one-step automation you run right there.
- Minute 5: the skill meter ticks for the first time.
- Right after the win: a 90-second placement staircase, 3 to 5 questions, each harder, stop at the first miss. It puts you on a rung of the topic's five-rung ladder (agents: Tourist, Tinkerer, Builder, Operator, Architect) and sets the starting difficulty. Shown as pride: "You're already a Tinkerer. Most people start as Tourists." A percentile appears only once 30 people have been placed on that topic.
- Only after that: "want tomorrow's?" Sign-in, a routine anchor ("after coffee / commute / lunch") and "why do you want this" in one sentence are asked here, once they're earned.
- Then, optional: "who do you already watch?" Paste a YouTube link or name a creator. Instagram links are accepted as caption-only hints and the UI says so.

Trade-off accepted: anonymous first sessions cost tokens with no account. Fine for the sprint, cap it later.

## The vision in the first 30 seconds: 7, 14, 28

Right after the topic is typed and the motivation is tapped, the learner sees three wins written for their topic and their reason. The roadmap is 28 days with boss days at 7, 14 and 28. Each day's session is generated the night before from how the previous days went, so there's no cost to the full 28. The sprint proves days 1 to 7 with real people; days 8 to 28 run untested until someone reaches them.

Motivation, one tap, four options (MECE on "why do you want this"):
1. Get ahead at work: promotion, visibility, being the one who knows.
2. Build something of my own: a side project, a product, money that isn't a salary.
3. For the joy of it: curiosity, a hobby, impressing friends.
4. Run my life better: time, money, health, home, family.

Writing rule: the vision sells pride, vanity and sloth, aimed at the chosen motivation. Each horizon is a win other people notice, stated as a fact about you, with the effort mentioned once and made small. Name real things: start from the one example everyone knows, end at what marks an expert. Never describe the work or the topics. Never "you'll learn." Always "you can," "people will," "while you."
- Day 7 (pride): something you can do that most people around you can't. 20 minutes a day, that's it.
- Day 14 (vanity): something others see and ask about. You're the one they come to.
- Day 28 (status, sloth): a thing that works for you, and you get the credit. Still 20 minutes a day, no homework.

No-code AI agents, by motivation:

Get ahead at work
- Day 7: An agent reads your Gmail every morning and drafts replies to the three that matter, built in n8n with zero code. Most people in your office still think that needs an engineer.
- Day 14: Your Monday report pulls from Slack, Sheets and the CRM and writes itself before you log in. Your manager asks how. Your name comes up in a meeting you're not in.
- Day 28: You chain agents that research, write and check each other, with an approval step, and you can explain why that beats one big prompt. You're the person people message when they want something automated.

Build something of my own
- Day 7: A bot that watches five subreddits and messages you every post where someone's asking for the thing you want to sell. Running tonight, no code.
- Day 14: Your landing page replies to every enquiry in your voice within a minute, books the call, and logs it. People think you have a team.
- Day 28: Your side project runs on three agents and takes you an hour a week. You've shipped something people pay for, and you did it between dinner and bed.

For the joy of it
- Day 7: A bot that texts you one thing you'll love every morning: a concert on sale, a recipe from your saved reels, a book your friend just rated five stars. You built it. No code.
- Day 14: Your friends ask you to make them one. You do it in ten minutes while they watch.
- Day 28: You've got a small fleet of agents that handle your hobbies, and you're the person at dinner explaining what an agent actually is, with examples you built.

Run my life better
- Day 7: An agent reads every school email and WhatsApp notice and puts the dates, fees and things to buy on the family calendar. You stop being the person who forgets.
- Day 14: Your bills, renewals and subscriptions get checked weekly and you get one message: what's due, what went up, what to cancel. Your partner notices.
- Day 28: Groceries, appointments, the car, the house: agents chase them and you approve. You've got evenings back, and it cost you 20 minutes a day for a month.

Art history, two motivations to show the pattern:

For the joy of it
- Day 7: You can say why the Mona Lisa is actually famous (the theft, the smile technique, the 1911 headlines), what makes a Caravaggio a Caravaggio, and why the Sistine ceiling nearly broke Michelangelo. Three stories nobody at the table knows.
- Day 14: Your friends ask you to come to the museum. You walk them from Giotto to Botticelli to Leonardo and explain what changed between each one, no labels.
- Day 28: You can place any painting in its period on sight, Renaissance to Baroque, Impressionism to Cubism to Abstract Expressionism, and say who broke what rule. It cost you less time than your Instagram scroll.

Get ahead at work (say, a consultant or marketer)
- Day 7: You open a client presentation with the story of why the Mona Lisa went viral in 1911, and the room is yours.
- Day 14: You're the person on the team who can pull a reference from Vermeer or Rothko into a brand deck and make it land. People ask where you studied.
- Day 28: You can walk a client through a gallery dinner, from Impressionism to Cubism, and explain what each movement broke. Nobody can tell it took 20 minutes a day.

Generated by one Claude call from the topic and the motivation, streamed while the first task loads. The call is told to name only things it's sure about. Cheap, so it's in the sprint.

## The session is a card stack (feels like Instagram, works like a tutor)

Each session is 10 to 12 cards, vertical swipe, one idea per card, mixed on purpose. A tutor can be summoned on any card ("ask"). The feed format is borrowed from Instagram; the habit it builds is not, because every third or fourth card demands something back.

Card types:
- Hook: one surprising line that opens the day.
- Story: a short narrative. The Mona Lisa theft, the first agent that emailed a client by mistake.
- Clip: a creator's video, trimmed to the 60 seconds that matter, credited.
- Guess first: "what do you think happens?" before the explanation.
- Prose: three sentences that teach the thing.
- Recall: one question from a previous day (spacing).
- Funny: a meme, a joke, a wrong answer from the internet. Breath, not content.
- Do: the task. Leaves the app if it has to.
- Teach back: explain it in your words, graded by the tutor.
- Boss: on 7, 14, 28. The result, then the share card.

Generator rules: no two cards of the same type in a row, no type more than 3 times. At least 4 demand cards (guess, recall, do, teach back, boss) and at least half the stack. Passive cards (prose, story, clip) at most 4. One funny card max, and it must carry content. Guess-first always before the prose on the same concept. Open with a hook, close with a test. Full rules in docs/adaptive-engine.md.

Why this helps the build: a card is one data shape (type plus content). Claude generates a stack. The screen is one swipeable component with a renderer per type.

## Binge mode: always allowed

"Next session" is always available. Spacing is protected by the clock, not by a gate.
- Session generation runs on demand, not only at night.
- Spacing reviews are scheduled by elapsed time. A binger who does days 1 to 4 on Saturday still gets the day 1 review on Monday as a nudge.
- The streak counts days, not sessions. Binging doesn't inflate it.
- Boss days unlock by passing tests, not by the calendar. "7 days in a weekend" is a share card line.
- After three sessions in a row, one line: "you'll remember more if you stop here," with a "keep going" button. No lock.
- Guardrail: binge completions followed by low scores on the later spaced reviews. The tutor says so and re-teaches.

## Pedagogy (the part that has to be excellent)

The rules below are well established in learning science. The plan applies them, it doesn't just name them.

| Principle | What it means | How day-to-day looks |
|---|---|---|
| Backward design | Start from the day 7 result, work back to what each day must teach | The first 30 seconds show the finish line. Every day's lesson is "one step closer to that" |
| Retrieval practice | Recalling beats rereading | Every session opens with 3 questions from yesterday before anything new |
| Spacing | Review at equal gaps, by the clock | Next day, then every 5 to 6 days. Learned means one correct recall in each of 3 later sessions |
| Interleaving | Mix old and new in one session | 70% new, 30% old, shuffled |
| Desirable difficulty | Hard enough to need effort, not so hard you quit | Tutor targets 85% success on demand cards. Above 90% level up, below 75% level down. Adjusted silently |
| Generation effect | Try first, then get taught | Each concept starts with "guess how this works" before the explanation |
| Elaboration | Explain it in your own words | One "teach it back" message per day. The tutor grades it |
| Worked examples then fading | Show a full example, then remove steps | Day 1 fully shown, by day 5 the learner does it alone |
| Immediate, specific feedback | Why it's wrong, not just that it's wrong | Tests never say "incorrect." They say what you confused it with |
| Metacognition | Know what you know | Confidence rating before each answer. Overconfident misses get flagged |

## Motivation and psychology

Self-determination theory says people stay when they feel competent, in control and connected. Each of the three gets one mechanic.

- Competence: a visible skill meter that only moves on tested evidence, never on watching.
- Autonomy: learner picks the time of day, the format (watch, read, do) and the day 7 project from three options.
- Relatedness: the tutor has a consistent voice and remembers things you said. Later, a cohort of people on the same path.

Other levers we use on purpose:
- Show the future first. The first 30 seconds are a demo of what day 7 produces. Curiosity gap, then the path to close it.
- Goal gradient. Meter starts at 2 of 28, never 0. Days 1 to 14 show "X done," days 15 to 28 show "Y left."
- Commitment device. Learner picks their time slot and says why they want this, right after the first win. The tutor quotes it back on a bad day.
- Variable reward. Not every session has a badge. Some have a surprise: a bonus clip, a harder challenge, a creator shout-out.
- Fresh start. A missed day triggers "today is a fresh start, here's a 5-minute catch-up," not guilt.
- Loss aversion, used gently. Two streak freezes at sign-up, auto-applied, earn-back within 48 hours. After a true break: "day 1 again, 9 days of skill kept," never a zero.

## Gamification (what earns what)

| Element | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Skill meter and ladder rung | Both move only when a test is passed. Rungs: five named levels per topic, set by placement | Competence must be real or it means nothing |
| Daily streak | Counts days. Two freezes at sign-up, max 2, auto-applied, never purchasable. Earn-back within 48 hours | Habit, with mercy |
| Badges | Only for what a test can't see: came back after a miss, boss first try. Max one a day, given after the act | Expected rewards for finishing cards undermine motivation |
| Challenge mode | Opt-in harder test, double meter gain | Autonomy and desirable difficulty |
| Boss day | 7, 14, 21 (mini), 28. A performance or project, never a quiz. Three attempts: hint after 1, half example after 2 | The result is the reward. Day 21 exists because engagement dips in week 4 |
| Share card | Day 7 output as an image, credits the creators | Distribution, and the creator pitch |

What we deliberately leave out: leaderboards (demotivate the bottom half), coins and shops (feel cheap), daily XP for watching (rewards passivity).

## The 28-day arc (first seven in detail)

| Day | Job | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | First win, no sign-up | Type the topic, tap why, see the 7/14/28 vision for that reason, do one tiny real task in 5 minutes, meter ticks. Then sign in, pick your slot, say why |
| 2 | Build the core | Retrieval from day 1, two new concepts, generation-first. Tutor sets your level from how day 1 and 2 went |
| 3 | Connect | Interleave days 1 and 2, first teach-back |
| 4 | Stretch | Hardest day. Challenge mode offered. Spacing review of day 1 |
| 5 | Apply | Mini project with faded support. Tutor only answers questions |
| 6 | Consolidate | Mixed test on everything, weak spots re-taught, project prep |
| 7 | Boss day 1 | Do the thing (build, perform, judge). Share card. Meter moves a band. Day 8 is generated tonight like any other |
| 8 to 13 | Same loop, harder | Generated night by night. Spacing reviews of week one woven in |
| 14 | Boss day 2 | The social win from the vision. Share card |
| 15 to 20 | Same loop | Generated night by night |
| 21 | Mini-boss | Keeps week 4 from sagging |
| 22 to 27 | Same loop | Generated night by night |
| 28 | Boss day 3 | The identity win from the vision. Share card. Skill meter reaches 1 |

Each day is 20 minutes, delivered as a card stack: recall cards first, then the mix, then do and test. Day 1 is shorter: 5 minutes to the first win, then up to 15 more if they want it.

## One-week build scope (sprint)

Stack is fixed: Convex (database, functions, auth, hosting), Claude for the tutor, public YouTube embeds for creator content. One topic this week.

| Day | Build | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | First session with no sign-up: topic in, one-tap motivation, 7/14/28 vision streams, first tiny task, meter ticks. Then Convex auth, slot and why | A stranger gets a first win in 5 minutes, then signs in and sees the 7-day path |
| Tue | Path generator for any topic, three calls: roadmap (day 28 result first, threshold concepts, rubrics), daily card stack, grader. QC judge on every day. YouTube search and transcripts for clips; "paste a link" and "name a creator." Hand-check the first task and day 7 result for agents | The agents topic gets a roadmap and a day 1 that passes QC and a verified first task |
| Wed | Card stack screen: swipeable, one renderer per card type, tutor summonable on any card, test with confidence rating and specific feedback. Claude generates the stack with the variety rules | A learner swipes through day 1 end to end, at least three cards made them do something |
| Thu | Adaptation loop: Beta per card type with delayed-recall reward, 85% difficulty staircase, SM-2 per concept, "more / less like this." Skill meter, day-based streak, 2 freezes, earn-back | Day 2 visibly differs after a bad day 1, and the card mix shifts by session 3 |
| Fri | Proactive layer: scheduled Convex job sends one nudge a day at last session time minus 30 minutes (email first, WhatsApp if time). Stops after 7 misses. Bad-day 3-minute card | Learner gets a message without opening the app, timed to when they actually showed up |
| Sat | Boss day screen (reused for 7, 14, 21, 28) and share card. 28-day roadmap view. Badges. Polish | One full 7-day run completed by you on agents, day 8 generates |
| Sun | Deploy with npm run deploy. Five real users through the first session. Fix what breaks | Five strangers on the path, nudges firing |

Out of scope for the sprint: testing days 8 to 28 with real people (they generate, nobody reaches them this week), creator licensing and revenue share, cohorts, Instagram saved-reel import, mobile app, leaderboards, more than one language (English only, Hindi next).

## Topic for the sprint: one

Build your first AI agent with no code. All five users on it. The generator is topic-agnostic (roadmap, day, grader), so opening topics later is a config change. No featured list and no "any skill" marketing this week. Art history stays in this doc as the worked example that proves the vision and result templates generalise.

## Success metrics (output, input, secondary, anti)

- Output: day 7 completion rate among people who reached the first win. Target 30% in week one.
- Input: time to first win (target under 5 minutes). Share of first-session visitors who sign in after the win. Day 2 completion within 24 hours of day 1. CURR: active today and at least once in the last 6 days. Sessions started from a nudge vs opened cold. Test pass rate between 60% and 95%.
- Secondary: share cards posted. "What's next" taps on day 7.
- Anti (guardrails): binge completions with low scores on later spaced reviews. Streaks kept by freezes rather than sessions. Meter moving without tests. Average session over 35 minutes (too heavy). Tests passed with low confidence (guessing).

## Kill criteria

- If fewer than 2 of 5 visitors reach the first win, or fewer than half of those sign in, the first session is wrong. Fix before anything else.
- If learners who got a nudge complete at the same rate as those who opened cold, proactive isn't the edge we think. Rethink.
