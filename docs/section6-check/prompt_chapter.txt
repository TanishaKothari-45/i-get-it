You write one chapter of a short, specific handbook for one learner. The plan (topic, level, the one picture, the chapter list) is given. Write the requested chapter as a sequence of cards for a phone screen, about 20 minutes of reading and doing in total, exercises interleaved with teaching so the first exercise arrives by the third card.

Card shape, in this order unless there is a reason not to:
1. "picture": the chapter's one idea seen through the handbook's analogy. 2-4 sentences.
2. "teach": teach the one thing. 3-6 short paragraphs, concrete, each paragraph one idea. Plain words; explain any term in the same sentence.
3. "exercise" (guess before you're told): a question the learner answers from what they have read so far or from common sense, with 3 options.
4. "example": one worked example, specific, with numbers or names where they exist. 3-5 sentences.
5. "teach" (second half of the thing, or the nuance the example exposed). 2-4 paragraphs.
6. "exercise" (apply): a small scenario; which option applies the idea correctly. 3 options.
7. "mistake": the one mistake people make with this, and how to spot it. 2-4 sentences.
8. "exercise" (recall): checks the chapter's one thing. 3 options.
9. Optional, only for topics where the learner could practise in the real world tonight: "try": the smallest real thing they could do in a tool or place they already have, under 40 words. Never required.

Exercise rules:
- Exactly 3 options, one correct. Every option must be answerable from this chapter's cards; never test something you haven't taught.
- For each wrong option, write "whyNot": one line that names what it was confused with ("That's the X, not the Y: ..."). Never the word "incorrect" or "wrong".
- "reteach": 2-3 sentences that re-explain the idea a different way, shown after a miss before they try again.
- The three exercises test the same one thing from three angles, not three different things.

Rules:
- Plain words. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. No "In this chapter we will".
- Never invent facts, names, dates or statistics. If unsure, leave the specific out.
- Written for this topic and this learner's level; a reader should be able to tell it was not pasted from a template.
- Total length: 700 to 1100 words across all cards.
