Write the requested chapter as a sequence of cards for a phone screen, about 20 minutes of reading and doing in total, exercises interleaved with teaching so the first exercise arrives by the third card.

Card shape, in this order unless there is a reason not to (10 cards, short ones; the first check arrives by card 2):
1. "picture": the chapter's one idea seen through the handbook's analogy, with one concrete image the reader can see. 2-3 sentences.
2. "exercise" (guess before you're told): a question the reader can answer from the picture card or common sense. 3 options. Make the scenario specific and a little playful.
3. "teach": teach the first half of the one thing. 2-3 short paragraphs, 60-110 words in total, each paragraph one idea. Plain words; explain any term in the same sentence.
4. "example": one worked example, specific, with names, numbers or places where they exist. Allowed, and welcome, to be dry-funny or surprising: a real-feeling moment, not a joke for its own sake. 3-5 sentences.
5. "exercise" (apply): a small scenario; which option applies the idea correctly. 3 options.
6. "teach": the second half, or the nuance the example exposed. 2-3 short paragraphs, 60-110 words.
7. "mistake": the one mistake people make with this, told as a tiny story of someone making it, and how to spot it. 2-4 sentences.
8. "exercise" (recall): checks the chapter's one thing from a third angle. 3 options.
9. "teach" (titled "In one breath"): the whole chapter in 2 sentences the reader could say to a friend.
10. Optional, only for topics where the reader could practise in the real world tonight: "try": the smallest real thing they could do in a tool or place they already have, under 40 words. Never required.

Formatting inside bodies: use **bold** for the one idea of each card (one bolded phrase per card, at most two), *italics* for a term being introduced or a quiet aside, and "\n\n" between paragraphs. No headings, no bullet lists, no emoji.

Exercise rules:

- The three exercises test the same one thing from three angles, not three different things.

Rules:

- Never invent facts, names, dates or statistics. If unsure, leave the specific out.

- Total length: 600 to 900 words across all cards. No single card over 120 words. Short beats complete.
- Tone: a sharp, warm friend who knows the subject, not a textbook. One moment per chapter that makes the reader smile or sit up; the rest plain and quick.
