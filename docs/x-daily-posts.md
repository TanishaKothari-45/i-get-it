## Thu 8 Oct: AI is confidently wrong

Facts: your own phone test on 4 Oct, chess basics. The chapter called d8 a light square (it's dark) and put the knights on the wrong squares. Now every chapter written live gets a fact check before the reader sees it. On the chess chapter, the check caught all 7 problems. Measured on 6 Oct: on the two worst topics, false claims went from 4 to 0 per chapter.
Screenshot: none needed; the d8 line is the hook.
Question: How did it feel to catch your own product teaching something false, and what would you tell someone building on AI?

## Fri 9 Oct: the longest answer was right 64% of the time

Facts: a tester noticed the right quiz option was always the longest. You checked 350 quizzes: the right answer was the longest in 64% of them, against 33% by chance. A reader could pass without learning anything. The fix: all three options the same length and detail, and every existing quiz rebalanced.
Screenshot: one quiz before and after.
Question: What does this say about trusting AI-written tests, and how did you find out?
