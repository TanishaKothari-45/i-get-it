# Delivery review

**Scope:** Two plans and two delivered chapters are supplied: guitar chapter 5 and cover-letter chapter 3. I can assess both plans, but not certify the missing chapters or the complete handbooks. Predicted swipe-stop points below are reader judgments, not measured behaviour.

**Bottom line:** The cover-letter handbook has a sensible overall shape, but its final chapter substitutes confidence rhetoric for a complete submission check. The guitar plan does not yet deliver the requested first song, and the supplied rhythm chapter claims a tempo it never establishes. Both were generated after research failed.

## Handbook 1: “learn guitar”

### A. Shape

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| What the reader walks away with | **Wrong target.** “Play my first song in a month” becomes a seven-day chord-and-loop outcome. A progression can support a song, but it is not itself a complete song. No song is identified. |
| Breadth | The plan includes useful components—clean notes, chords, changes and rhythm—but omits an explicit tuning method, a usable string/fret reference, and the chosen song’s structure. Chapter 7 promises a “G-Em-C-D” progression without teaching G or C. |
| Depth | There is no research assessment. Several narrow techniques receive separate chapters, while the actual song is squeezed into a final loop chapter. Chapter 1 risks being a preparation-only sitting: posture and a clean note rather than an early musical result. |
| Pace | **Course is right:** finger placement and rhythm need practice between sittings. Seven chapters are defensible, but seven consecutive days are not an honest substitute for the reader’s month-long practice horizon. |
| Whole pieces and length | Chapter 5 can hold a complete steady-downstrum piece, but the delivered version lacks its timing setup and verification. Chapter 7 is not ready to use with the preceding chord coverage. |

**Right shape:** A practice-paced course of up to seven sittings spread across the month, built around one identified, beginner-suitable song, with tuning and orientation embedded in an early musical win.

### C. Never: errors and safety misses

#### Delivered cards

| Location | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Card 1 | “Your right elbow” excludes a left-handed setup without establishing handedness. | Say “strumming arm,” or establish the assumed setup and its left-handed equivalent. |
| Card 2 | “Across all six strings” is not a general downstrumming instruction suitable for all the chords already taught. D major requires avoiding the lowest two strings. | Specify the chord used for this drill and the strings to strike; distinguish the stroke direction from its string range. |
| Card 3 | “If your stroke sounds like slapping a fence… your wrist has frozen solid” presents one sound as proof of one cause. Pick depth, angle and grip can also cause snagging or harshness. | Describe wrist tension as one possible cause, then give an observable check and correction. |
| Cards 1–4 | There is no stop instruction beside practice involving tension. Card 3 describes a forearm that “knots,” but treats loosening the grip as the entire answer. | Tell the reader to stop and rest if there is pain or increasing strain, then resume gently only when comfortable. |
| Card 5 | “An unbroken elbow swing” is presented as the basis of clean rhythm; “so your pulse never falters” is an unsupported guarantee. Continuous timing does not require a literal continuous elbow swing. | Emphasise comfortable coordinated movement and an external beat; remove the guarantee. |
| Card 5 closing | “Next… simple chord shapes” does not lead to the planned **one-minute change drill**, and chord shapes have already been taught. | Tease timed changes, using the actual next plan entry. |
| Outcome line | “You can now… to a 60 BPM pulse” is not earned: no pulse is supplied or established. | Teach how to set and follow that pulse, then provide a check; otherwise narrow the outcome. |

#### Plan-level problems affecting delivery

- **Chapter 7:** “G-Em-C-D” includes two untaught chords. Teach every required chord before using it, or choose a progression using taught chords.
- **Chapter 3:** the shared-finger “anchor” is not established by explicit finger assignments. It may be possible with selected fingerings, but the plan cannot assume it follows from whatever chapter 2 teaches.
- **Outcome and chapter 7 hook:** “By day 7” mastery and a rule that “guarantees” the first song sounds musical are unearned promises.
- **Missing end product:** a four-measure loop is labelled as the destination without identifying or teaching a complete song.

**Foreign defaults:** None seen in names, money, units or institutions. The handedness assumption is an accessibility/setup issue, not an Indian-localisation error.

**Prohibited personal advice or harmful instruction:** None seen.

### D. Verdict

**No, on the supplied evidence:** this reader may learn useful fragments, but the plan does not provide a complete first song or a credible month-long path to playing it.

## 2. Shape across the set

There is **no evidence that everything gets the same chapter count**: guitar gets seven, cover letter three. The quick/course distinction also fits these two goals.

There **is** evidence of a default shape **within each topic category**:

- Every guitar chapter uses `move, doit, mistake, doit, breath`.
- Every cover-letter chapter uses `teach, example, mistake, tryit, breath`.

The code requests one retry for identical lists, but the supplied plans still contain them. The material does not show the retry result or whether repeated sameness then passes; that remains uncertain.

**What gets squeezed:** the guitar’s actual song, prerequisite coverage and month-long practice path.

**What gets stretched:** the cover letter’s polishing rhetoric and repeated confidence corrections.

The length mechanism also pulls against depth-led shape:

> “60 words a minute, 300 to 1,200”

Reading-and-doing minutes are not writing minutes. A physical drill can take several minutes with very little text. Conversely, a complicated procedure may need more instruction than that conversion allows. The supplied guitar chapter is much shorter than the stated floor, but the material does not establish whether that resulted from generation, processing or another step.

## 4. Top seven fixes, ranked

### 1. Stop treating failed research as a successful grounding step
**Step: code**

**Change:**
> “If research fails, the plan is told there is no brief and decides alone.”

**Replacement behaviour:** Retry the research step with the validation error. If it still fails, distinguish failure from a valid brief with few facts. Generate a conservative fallback only for content that can be supported, clearly narrowing uncertain claims; do not publish actionable high-risk or unverifiable current content without adequate grounding.

This prevents the same silent failure from reaching every later step.

### 2. Validate the goal-to-plan path before chapters are written
**Step: plan + code**

**Change the outcome rule:**
> “The outcome is specific and honest… (‘By day N you'll…’ for a course of N chapters…).”

**Replacement wording:**
> “State an observable end result that matches the reader’s goal. Chapter count is not time to mastery. Use a deadline only when the plan and practice requirements support it. Before returning, check that the final deliverable is present, every prerequisite is taught before use, every needed piece is covered, and no chapter outcome promises an external response.”

**Code change:** Run this coverage check on the completed plan, not just its chapter count and block lists. Reject or repair missing prerequisites and mismatched deliverables before generation.

### 3. Give the writer enough freedom to finish the promised piece
**Step: writer**

**Change:**
> “Build the chapter from those blocks, in that order; you may add one teach card between two blocks where an idea needs it, and nothing else.”

**Replacement wording:**
> “Use the planned blocks as a starting structure. Add, split, remove or reorder cards when needed to deliver the promised pieces completely and safely. Preserve chapter-1 quiz restrictions. Include the necessary setup, instructions, checks, exceptions and recovery steps; do not add cards merely for variety.”

This addresses both missing rhythm instructions and incomplete submission checks without requiring longer chapters by default.

### 4. Separate reading length from practice time
**Step: code + writer**

**Code change:** Remove the automatic `minutes × 60` word target and the universal 300–1,200-word range. Pass piece depth, expected reading time and expected doing time separately.

**Change:**
> “Write about that many words across all cards.”

**Replacement wording:**
> “Use the shortest complete treatment of these pieces for this reader. Practice time does not require more prose. Split cards where necessary for one idea per card; stop when the promised content and checks are complete.”

Also replace the four-non-quiz-card failure threshold with a completeness test. A card-count floor is not evidence of a useful sitting.

### 5. Carry forward a compact record of what was actually taught
**Step: code**

**Change:**
> “The writer never sees earlier chapters’ text.”

**Replacement behaviour:** Pass a concise ledger of taught terms, prerequisite steps, selected options, examples, established analogy mappings and attained capabilities. Include the exact next plan entry.

Check the closing line against that next entry; on the last chapter, check it against the actual delivered capabilities. This supports continuity without passing every earlier word.

### 6. Remove attention rules that conflict with safety or direct teaching
**Step: writer**

**Change:**
> “A cost appears only inside a mistake card’s scene, never as a warning to the reader…”

**Replacement wording:**
> “State a relevant downside or precaution directly where the reader needs it, before action when necessary. Never delay it for a scene or suspense. Keep the consequence proportionate.”

**Also change:**
> “One moment in each chapter makes the reader smile.”

**To:**
> “Use humour only when it naturally helps this topic and reader. Otherwise use warmth or straightforward teaching.”

Treat body-skill pictures, scenes and movement cues as optional teaching tools, not mandatory drama or literal mechanical models.

### 7. Expand fact checking into a delivery gate, and close its enforcement gaps
**Step: fact check + code**

**Change:**
> “Internal consistency: does any card contradict another card?”

**Replacement wording:**
> “Check consistency with the chapter plan and taught-content ledger as well as other cards. Does the chapter supply every required instruction, check and safety condition? Is its outcome earned? Does its closing match the next chapter or the completed handbook? Flag missing content even when the existing sentences are true.”

**Code changes:**
- Normalise `new` and `complete beginner` so the beginner check cannot silently depend on different labels. The supplied material does not show whether this already happens.
- Permit a checker to request a missing card or return a chapter for revision; “same type and shape” fixes cannot repair every omission.
- Do not release a chapter with unresolved critical issues as merely **“unchecked.”**
- Verify required quizzes rather than silently dropping malformed ones. Keep the spec’s exemptions—chapter 1 and recaps—but do not make all quick handbooks exempt solely because they are quick.
- Before reusing a shared handbook, verify relevant level, language, jurisdiction and time-sensitive context. The described matching only establishes topic and goal; other matching may exist, but is not shown.

## Separate notes on the spec

1. **“Never wrong” lacks a user-visible uncertainty policy.** The spec should say what happens when evidence is unavailable, disputed or stale, rather than relying only on omission.
2. **The three-chapter minimum can conflict with “never padding” for genuinely tiny requests.** These examples do not prove that conflict, but it needs a stated resolution.
3. **“Passed” needs a clearer meaning.** Logged repetitions or a ticked checklist demonstrate participation, not necessarily competence. The rung should name what was demonstrated, not automatically claim mastery.
4. **The promised app flow is not fully assessable here.** No evidence shows chapter-1 latency, “tonight” labelling, rung feedback or actual interactive-page behaviour. Those require a delivery review of the app, not just its chapter text.
