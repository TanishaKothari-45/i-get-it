## Handbook 1: "learn guitar"

Goal: play my first song in a month. Level: complete beginner. Plan: course, 7 chapters, body skill.

### A. Shape

- **Walks away with:** the goal is a result: *a song*. The plan's outcome is something else: "switch cleanly between three essential open chords and play a complete 4-beat song progression". No chapter names a song, and none ends with one. Chapter 7 delivers "an unbroken 4-measure chord loop", which is an exercise and not a song. The plan has swapped the reader's goal for a generic skill.
- **Pieces:** most of the right pieces are there: posture, two-finger chords, changes, one more chord, strumming, a change drill, a loop. Two that a complete beginner needs are missing:
  - **Tuning.** An untuned guitar makes every "clean note" check in chapter 1 meaningless. This is a prep step that belongs inside chapter 1.
  - **Holding a pick, or choosing not to use one.** Chapter 5 talks about "snagging the pick" and "loosen your grip", but no chapter teaches the pick.
- **Dependencies are broken.** Chapter 7 plays "a classic G-Em-C-D style beginner progression", but **G and C are never taught**. The chords taught are Em, A7 and D. The outcome itself says "three essential open chords", and chapter 7 needs four, two of them new. This is the most serious plan error in the set.
- **Order:**
  - Strumming arrives in chapter 5. Chapter 3's outcome already asks the reader to switch chords "in rhythm".
  - Chapter 2 promises chords that "let you make music right away", but the strumming hand isn't taught until three days later.
  - Chapter 4's "thumb muting" (thumb over the neck) conflicts with chapter 1's "thumb placement behind the neck". It is also more than a complete beginner needs for D: picking out only the right strings is enough.
- **Depth and targets:**
  - Chapter 6's "at least 25 clean chord switches in sixty seconds between Em and D" is a number with no brief behind it. It is also a stretch on day 6 for a complete beginner. The writer's rule says targets must let "a beginner succeed the first time".
  - Chapter 2's hook "dozens of recognizable folk and pop tunes" is also unsourced.
- **Pace and count:** course is right, because the reader must practise between sittings. Seven chapters is defensible for a month's goal. "By day 7" compresses a one-month goal into a week, though, and nothing in the plan tells the reader the other three weeks are for practice.
- **Right shape, in one line:** 6 to 7 chapters, all built around one real beginner song:
  1. Tune, hold, and strum your first chord, so music happens on night 1.
  2. Steady downstrums on that chord.
  3. to 5. The song's own chords, each with its change.
  6. The change drill.
  7. The song, start to finish.

## Handbook 2: "write a cover letter for my first job"

Goal: send a strong cover letter this week. Level: complete beginner. Plan: quick, 3 chapters (12, 15 and 10 minutes).

### A. Shape

- **Walks away with:** a result, a letter ready to send. The outcome ("a tailored, three-paragraph cover letter ready to submit") matches the goal.
- **Pieces:** the opening, research, evidence, closing and checklist are right. For "send this week", these are missing:
  - **The letter's frame.** Greeting (who to address when the post names no one), sign-off, name and contact block, and length (one page).
  - **How it is sent.** Email body or attachment, subject line, portal upload. Many first-job applications in India go by email, LinkedIn or Naukri, not as a PDF to a named manager.
  - **The whole letter assembled once.** Chapter 3's outcome says the reader can "assemble the complete letter", but no card shows a full letter or the order it goes in.
- **Depth and pace:** quick is right for "this week", and 3 chapters is right. The 37 minutes fits one or two sittings.
- **Right shape, in one line:** 3 chapters as planned. Chapter 1 adds the frame (greeting, length, where the letter goes). Chapter 3 adds the sign-off, sending, and one complete example letter.

### 4. Top 7 fixes, ranked

1. **Code: never plan without research when research fails.**
   - Code: replace "If research fails, the plan is told there is no brief and decides alone" with one retry of research. On the retry, keep the same prompt and model and, if the failure was the provider being overloaded, wait before trying.
   - If the retry also fails, pass the plan the flag `Research: none`.
   - Plan prompt: after "Never add a number that the brief does not give.", add: *"With Research: none, put no counts, speeds, scores or targets in titles, hooks or outcomes, and no claim of how many or how often; describe the result in words, and name every setup step a complete beginner needs before the first action."*

2. **Plan: the last chapter delivers the goal, and nothing is used before it is taught.** Replace "Chapters build in order." with: *"Chapters build in order. A chapter uses only what it or an earlier chapter teaches: every shape, term, tool or step it needs is taught by then. The last chapter delivers the reader's goal itself, in the goal's own words: if the goal names something to play, make, send, fix or decide, the last chapter ends with the reader doing that whole thing. The outcome names that same thing."*

3. **Plan: stop copying the default block lists.**
   - Plan prompt: replace "Rough defaults, not rules:" with *"Starting points only; each chapter's blocks follow its own pieces, and no two chapters share the same list unless their content truly repeats:"*.
   - Plan prompt: in the body default, after "move, doit, mistake, doit, breath", add *"with a teach card before the first move whenever the move needs exact placements (which finger, where, how far, how fast) that 2 or 3 cues cannot hold, and an opening line that pays off the chapter's hook"*.
   - Code: if the retry still returns identical lists, store the plan with a flag the eval can see, not silently.

4. **Writer and fact check: the outcome line must be earned.**
   - Writer, add under Rules: *"Every part of this chapter's outcome (each number, tool, step or result in it) must be taught or done on a card. Teach what is missing; never claim in the outcomeLine what the cards did not deliver."*
   - Fact check, add a bullet: *"Outcome: does the outcomeLine claim a number, step or result that no card teaches? Then the outcome is the problem: return a fixed card that teaches it, or say so."*
   - If code copies the plan outcome into outcomeLine, have it use the writer's line instead.

5. **Code and writer: continuity with earlier chapters.**
   - Code: add an `Already can:` line to each chapter request, listing the outcomes of every earlier chapter, and a `Next chapter:` line with its title and covers.
   - Writer, add: *"The reader can already do everything in 'Already can': practise the new thing with it (hold a shape they know, use the draft they wrote), never re-teach or contradict it. The 'Next:' line describes the next chapter as 'Next chapter' states it."*

6. **Research and plan: setup steps a beginner lacks.**
   - Research: in DECIDE, extend "start" to *"...Chapter 1 starts there. Also name any setup the reader needs before the first real action that this Level suggests they lack (a tool set up, an app, a document, an account), so the planner can place it."*
   - Plan: after "put it inside the chapter that needs it", add *"(name it in that chapter's covers)"*.
   - The frame and sending of a written result (greeting, sign-off, where it goes) count as setup pieces of the result.

7. **Writer: honest tone, and varied names.**
   - Writer, under VOICE AND TENSION, add: *"No counts of other people you can't support ('most applicants', 'hundreds of'), no promise of an outcome the reader doesn't control (an interview, a job, a sale), and no cost words ('disqualify', 'ruthless') outside a mistake scene."*
   - Code: pass 4 Indian first names, chosen at random from a varied list, as `Names to use:` in each chapter request, so the same invented name doesn't turn up in every handbook.

### Notes on the spec

- **No rule for when research fails.** The spec doesn't say whether a handbook may ship with no brief. Section 4's "Never wrong" is hard to meet without one. Say whether to retry, block, or mark the handbook.
- **Long goals and short courses.** The spec doesn't say how a goal with a long horizon ("in a month") relates to a course read one chapter a day. Say whether the outcome must cover the whole horizon, or whether practice days are named.
- **Continuity between chapters is missing.** Section 3 judges each chapter alone. Add: "a chapter uses what earlier chapters taught and never contradicts them."
- **Result goals.** For a goal whose deliverable is a thing (a letter, a dish, a song), the spec could say the reader sees or makes the whole thing at least once.
- **"No shape is the default" versus the prompt.** Section 2 says no shape is the default, but the plan prompt hands over default block lists that end up copied verbatim. The spec could say that variety across chapters is part of "chapter length follows depth".
